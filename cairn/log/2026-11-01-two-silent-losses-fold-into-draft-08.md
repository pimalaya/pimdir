# Two silent losses fold into draft-08

Two items cairn/changes/the-consumers-gaps-fold-into-draft-08/tasks.md left open lost a body nobody chose to lose: a queued `update` predating a conflict settled it on drain, and a write finding its name held by a part a crashed copy left kept the part over the only good bytes. The user decided both rules on 2026-11-01 and to fold them into storage-draft-08 and sync-draft-07, no number bump, as the-consumers-gaps-fold-into-draft-08 did: neither is tagged, no schema moves, and checks/tags.sh reads both as "no tag yet" (cairn/changes/two-silent-losses-fold-into-draft-08, whose proposal says why each choice below was made).

Rules, and the check guarding each:

- **A resolution names its conflict** (SYNC §7, normative). An `Edit` without `overrule` settles a conflict only as a person's resolution stating `resolves`, the diverging bodies it was decided over: it meets the item's conflict when `items.conflicted` is 1 and its binding's when `bindings.conflicted` is 1, and meeting either is refused, staging nothing, unless `resolves` names the `conflict_object` of each; stating `resolves` and meeting none it is refused the same way, the conflict it decided being settled already; a conflict whose `conflict_object` is `NULL`, a body nobody has read, is named by none. The `overrule` bullet now says a person's resolution states `resolves`, and the person's settle is "with `resolves`". An ordinary edit, made before a conflict arose or without it in view, so never discards a diverging body. Guarded by vectors/sync/124 (an ordinary edit over the item conflict of 118, c4 over c3: refused, the conflict over c2 and both bodies kept), 125 (`resolves` naming c4, an earlier conflict's body), 126 (`resolves` over an item in no conflict), 127 (an ordinary edit through b's binding conflict of 103), 128 (`resolves` over the unread conflict of 101) and 129 (`resolves` naming the item's c2 but not b's c4, the store of 105), each `failed` with its rows unchanged; 28, 85, 86, 103, 104 and 118 to 123, every person's settle the vectors held, now state `resolves` and keep their expectations. checks/vectors.py checks every mutate case against the rule whatever an engine does: an `Edit` without `overrule` meeting a conflict its `resolves` does not name, or stating it over none, expects `failed` and its items and bindings as they were. Seeded against 118 with its `resolves` removed: the check fails on the expected `failed` and on both tables.
- **The update's `resolves`** (STORAGE §15.2, §15.3, normative). The `update` shape at v 1 adds `"resolves": [hash…]?`, a non-empty array of distinct object names, the notation gaining `[hash…]`. A producer deciding a conflict (a resolution command or form) states the `conflict_object` of each conflict it showed, read from `list_conflicted_items` and `list_conflicted_bindings`, and MUST NOT state it on any other update. The drain parks an update meeting a conflict, naming it, unless `resolves` names each conflict's body, and one stating `resolves` over none; a resolution runs through a conflicted binding whose body it names when the item has one, since the producer cannot know which binding the drain picks. An unbound item's `update` takes `resolves` on the same terms. payloads/update.v1.schema.json defines the member (`minItems` 1, `uniqueItems`, each a name); vectors/payloads.json gains two valid cases (one name with `if_match`, two names) and five malformed ones (an empty array, a bare name, a name twice, a path, `true`), its note naming len-2's body beside the empty body's and len-1's. checks/vectors.py's shape reader takes `[hash…]`; without the placeholder STORAGE §15.3's shape does not read, and without the schema's member the shape and the schema differ.
- **A payload shape moves in place while its part is a draft** (STORAGE §15.3, normative). §15.3 has a member added take the next `v`, which would have made `resolves` an `update` v 2. While the part is a draft a payload's shape at its `v` is now edited in place, as version 1 of the schema is (§4.2), a draft's store opening under that draft alone. The schemas being closed, a program of storage-draft-08 before this fold parks an update stating `resolves` as malformed rather than settle the conflict, and its own updates, stating none, park over a conflict under a program after it: neither mix loses a body. No check reads the sentence.
- **A name held is verified** (STORAGE §5, §7, normative). A writer finding its final name held, or skipping its write because it finds the name or its row there, MUST verify the file there against its name before keeping it: one that passes is kept; one that fails an owner moves into quarantine/ as §7 does, under the staging lock taken exclusively, serialised against its own writers and verified again under the lock, then moves again; a producer, which moves nothing, fails its write, enqueues nothing and reports the name, its bytes staying with its caller until the owner frees the name. MUST rather than SHOULD because §7 already makes every whole read verify, and the write is the one moment the good bytes stand beside the failing ones; the cost is one whole read of the held file when a name is found held. §7's quarantine and producer bullets and §18's list of hostile-content rules follow. Guarded by vectors/sync/130, vector 30's Full upgrade over c2 whose file is `corrupt` and nothing has read: the item and the base name c2 and `expect.store.objects` states c2 with neither `blob` nor `corrupt`, so a write keeping the failing file fails it. SYNC §11 and vectors/README.md now say how an object stated after the run reads; the format already allowed it (`rows`), so the schema did not move for it.
- **The exclusive create and copy** (STORAGE §5, normative). The final name created with `O_CREAT | O_EXCL`, the temporary's bytes copied into it, the file `fsync`ed, the temporary unlinked: the one non-atomic no-replace move, which a writer MAY use only where no atomic one is offered or allowed. While the copy runs, and after a crash in it, the name holds part of the body, which a read finds failing and the next write of the body sets aside (above); a copy that fails unlinks the part it created, the temporary still holding the bytes. Write's first sentence no longer calls every move atomic. Program behaviour on a platform no check here runs.
