## Rules

- **A message's header is read one way** (STORAGE Annex A.0, A.1; I1). A.1 now states every parse the `message_id`, `in_reply_to`, `subject`, `sender_name`, `date` columns and the `alt:` key rest on, closest to RFC 5322 where a writer had to choose:
  - the header block, unfolding (a line break before whitespace removed, the whitespace kept), a field read from its first occurrence, its bytes read as UTF-8 (RFC 6532), comments and quoted-strings (RFC 5322 §3.2), whitespace the space and the tab;
  - an unstructured value (`Subject`, a display name): every RFC 2047 encoded word decoded wherever it stands; a charset known when it is a label of the WHATWG Encoding Standard other than its replacement encoding, decoded by that encoding, no byte order mark sniffed; a malformed `B` or `Q` word, or one in an unknown charset, kept as its literal text; whitespace between two decoded words removed and no other; the ends trimmed of spaces and tabs, inner whitespace kept;
  - a display name: the text before `<`, comments as spaces, each quoted-string its content, then decoded as unstructured; an address list split at the commas outside quotes, comments and angle brackets, a group listing its members;
  - `Message-ID`: comments skipped, the first `<` outside one to the next `>`, trimmed; bracketless, the whole field, comments removed and trimmed, when it holds no whitespace, no `<`, `>` or `"` and one `@`, else unparseable; case kept. `In-Reply-To` the same way, each id, a bracketless field word by word;
  - `Date`: RFC 5322 §3.3 with §4.3's obsolete forms: comments removed, the day of the week up to the first comma skipped and never checked, five words (whitespace around a colon not counting), the seconds optional, a two-digit year below 50 in the 2000s and one of 50 or more or a three-digit one in the 1900s, then 1900 to 9999; zones `+hhmm` and `-hhmm` (`mm` at most 59, `-0000` as `+0000`), `UT`, `GMT`, `EST`/`EDT`, `CST`/`CDT`, `MST`/`MDT`, `PST`/`PDT`, and a military letter other than `J` at `+0000` as RFC 5322 §4.3 reads it; a missing zone or anything after it unparseable; the day existing in its month, the hour 00 to 23, the minute 00 to 59, the second 00 to 60 with 60 read as 59, so `30 Feb` and `24:00` are unparseable (`NULL`), never carried into the next day.

  Guarded by vectors/summaries.json, eleven new cases over eleven new fixtures (CRLF, byte-exact, pinned by .gitattributes' `-text`): a Message-ID without brackets, a bracketless one naming nothing, comments around the ids (the one before the id holding a `<` of its own) with a commented `In-Reply-To` and a commented display name, an obsolete two-digit year (96), an obsolete zone name in a folded, commented Date with a year below 50, a military zone with a three-digit year, no seconds, 30 February, a folded and padded Subject joining two encoded words, two malformed words beside one in an unknown charset, and raw bytes that are no UTF-8; the six stating no Message-ID carry what they pin into their `alt:` key. checks/vectors.py now re-derives, for every mail case, `message_id`, `in_reply_to`, `subject`, `sender`, `sender_name`, `date` and the `sort_key`, by a parser written from A.1 (not the `email` package, lenient where the annex is exact), and the sync check of a message minted apart reads the member through it and compares `date` too. - **Bytes that are no UTF-8** (A.0; I1): each maximal subpart of an ill-formed sequence one U+FFFD, Unicode §3.9's practice and the WHATWG UTF-8 decoder's, which Python's `replace` handler follows. Guarded by the raw 8-bit case above: `E9` before a space one, `F0 9F 98` truncated one, `C3` before `(` one, `C0 AF` two, `ED A0 80` three.
- **The agreement rule holds where it can** (Annex A's preamble, A.1; I1). Of the two options the ledger allowed, the byte-for-byte MUST is restricted to what a server serves raw: a header field fetched as such (IMAP `HEADER.FIELDS`, Gmail's metadata headers, JMAP's `header:…:asRaw`, Graph's `internetMessageHeaders`) and an IMAP `ENVELOPE`'s date, subject and message-id strings, each read by A.1's parses. A value a server decodes or parses itself (JMAP's `subject` and `sentAt`, Graph's `subject` and `sentDateTime`, an `ENVELOPE` address's name) need not agree, and the body's derivation replaces it once read: without that clause a relisting would overwrite the body's columns with the server's reading. A link id derived from such a value is kept (identity is resolved once) and may key a message apart, a missed dedup, so a writer SHOULD read the hint's fields raw. The vectors match by construction: every member's meta in vectors/sync/ is read from its fixture, that is raw, and agrees with the body. No vector format carries a server-decoded value, so the replacement clause is unguarded.
- **A zoned time: database order and transitions** (STORAGE Annex A.3, SEARCH §7; I2). A `TZID` resolves through the resource's own `VTIMEZONE`, then the IANA time zone database (a resource may omit a standard zone's `VTIMEZONE`, RFC 7809), else as floating. A wall time a transition repeats or skips takes the UTC offset in effect before the transition, RFC 5545 §3.3.5: the earlier instant of an ambiguous one (unchanged), and a skipped one read at the offset before the gap, so `20260308T023000` in New York is `07:30:00Z` where the old rule (the numerically greater offset, the one after the gap) gave `06:30:00Z`. SEARCH §7 states the same for every occurrence, a recurrence instance's included (RFC 5545 §3.3.10). Guarded by vectors/summaries.json, the gap case fixed and its note rewritten, and a new case, event-zoned-iana.ics, a `TZID` with no `VTIMEZONE` that Paris's +02:00 resolves to `07:00:00Z` (floating would give `09:00:00Z`); checks/vectors.py now re-derives every event and task sort key through the resource's `VTIMEZONE` (a yearly rule by month and weekday, expanded by hand), else `zoneinfo` (nixpkgs' Python names its own tzdata store path, so the sandbox resolves zones), else floating. The tree before fails the new vectors.py at the gap case (`06:30:00Z is not 07:30:00Z`); seeded, floating Paris fails too. For the index, vectors/search/store.json gains event-zoned-gap.ics as item 26 and queries.json a window of 8 March holding its one occurrence, `07:30:00Z` to `07:30:00Z`: the event ending at 03:30 EDT spans nothing once its start is read at the offset before the gap. vectors.py checks that window's shape and order only; an indexer runs it. The two cases listing every calendar item or every item (`folder:home/Calendar`, `changed:..1000000000`) gain the item.
- **`seq` is shared within a kind** (STORAGE §9.1; I3). `seq_for_link_any(collection, link_id)` answers the `seq` of a row holding the link id in a collection of the kind of `:collection`, so a Message-ID spelling an event's UID draws its own `seq`, never one hit and one `id:` with the event; a message in two mailboxes, a retained copy included, and a `part:` file in two folders still share one. Its plan seeks the bound collection by its key, `items_by_link (link_id=?)`, and each holder's collection by its key, on 3.50.4. Guarded by invariants.sh, "seq: a Message-ID spelling an event's UID is another item, drawing its own seq" (before: `1`) and "seq: a message filed twice shares one, retained or not, and so does a part's file in two folders" (before: the calendar collection got the file's `3`), and the plan guard "seq: seq_for_link_any seeks the link id on items_by_link and each collection by its key, scanning nothing".
- **One table assigns a key; held is any row** (STORAGE §2, §9; I4). §2 and §9 define **held**: a key is held in a collection when a row of `items` there has it, live, tombstoned or retained (`seq_by_link` answers), free otherwise. §9 states minting (`dup:`, the key, `#`, the handle) and minting again over a held key with the same handle until free (`dup:dup:k#h#h`), which never lands, joins nor revives what holds the key it would mint. One table, keyed on what holds the key, gives the result for a member a source names, an `Add`, and a `Copy` or `Move`: free; a pending create of this source (landed); a based binding of this source under another handle (minted); a live item other sources alone bind, mail agreeing (joined) or disagreeing (minted); a live unbound item (joined); a tombstone still propagating (joined and revived, disagreeing mail minted; revived by a create); a retained row (revived). A policy's or an overruling `Edit`'s loser and a rekey are keyed outside it. Today's behaviour is stated as the parts had it; the live unbound row, which no branch named, is stated as the write merges it. STORAGE §11, §11.1, §13 (`link_id`), §15.3, SYNC §6, §7, §9 and GUIDE cite the table rather than restate it; SYNC keeps its sync-specific conditions (minting decided against the whole collection, in handle order; the write enforcing the mail agreement). Guarded by the S6 vectors below and two guards in invariants.sh: "held: seq_by_link answers a tombstoned and a retained row alike, and nothing for a free key" and "held: live_item_for_link answers neither, being the live test of the Add column, never the held one minting reads".
