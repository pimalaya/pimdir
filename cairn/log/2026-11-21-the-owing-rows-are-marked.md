# The owing rows are marked

storage-draft-15, not tagged, edited in place: no migration and no number moves. io-pimdir's cairn/log/2026-11-21-a-sync-that-finds-nothing.md left a no-op sync of the 517k Enron store at 0.49 s (notmuch 0.07 s), 0.43 s of it `list_owing_bindings` and `list_lacking_items` walking every binding and item of each collection to answer nothing. The owner chose on 2026-11-21 a mark backed by a partial index over a per-source stamp (cairn/changes/the-owing-rows-are-marked/proposal.md).

## Rules and the check guarding each

- **The owing mark** (STORAGE.md §4.6, §4.3): `bindings.owes`, the value of the view `binding_owes`, the predicate `list_owing_bindings` evaluated per binding before, recomputed by `bindings_owes_insert`, `bindings_owes_update` (key, conflict, base), `items_owes_update` (conflict, deletion, flag set, body), `placement_flag_owes_insert`/`_delete` and `binding_base_flag_owes_insert`/`_delete`. `list_owing_bindings` reads `owes = 1` on `bindings_owing(collection, source, handle)`.
- **The lacking mark** (§4.6, §4.3): `items.lacks`, the value of `item_lacks` (live, bodied, and a source of the collection's `sources` binds it not), recomputed by `items_lacks_insert`, `items_lacks_update` (collection, deletion, body), `bindings_lacks_insert`/`_delete`/`_update` and `sources_lacks_insert`/`_delete`/`_update`. `list_lacking_items` reads `lacks = 1` on `items_lacking(collection, link_id)` for a source with a row, and walks the collection for one without, under a one-row guard empty while the row exists.
- **A flag row is never rewritten in place** (§4.6): `placement_flag_keep_row` and `binding_base_flag_keep_row` refuse an update, which neither the marks' triggers nor the stamps' (§4.5) follow.
- **The delta's reads cost what is owed** (§14, SYNC.md §10, GUIDE.md).

Checks, all in checks/invariants.sh:

- the probe, run by `run` and `sql` after every statement of every scenario: each mark equals its view, every binding the statement before the marks names is marked, and every live bodied item a source with a row binds not is marked; the gate fails on any line it wrote;
- the scenario "The owing marks follow every write": each input of each rule moved by a canonical statement or a literal write, and after each the two statements answer, for two sources with a row and one without, what the statements before the marks answer (spelled in the scenario) and what the scenario states;
- the plan checks: `list_owing_bindings` seeks `bindings_owing` with no temporary b-tree; `list_lacking_items` seeks `items_lacking`, its walk nested under the guard; the owing rule (planned through `bindings_owes_insert`) and `evict_before` compare the flag sets by two anti-joins.

**Each fails without what it guards.** Twenty mutations of the tree (a script outside it writing one copy each), each run through the whole of checks/invariants.sh: every one of the seventeen triggers dropped, the view's base-keyword term removed, `list_lacking_items` without its walk, and `list_owing_bindings` reading `owes = 0`. All twenty fail, by these counts of broken invariants: `binding_base_flag_keep_row` 1, `binding_base_flag_owes_delete` 3, `binding_base_flag_owes_insert` 12, `bindings_lacks_delete` 3, `bindings_lacks_insert` 1 (the probe), `bindings_lacks_update` 3, `bindings_owes_insert` 6, `bindings_owes_update` 13, `items_lacks_insert` 3, `items_lacks_update` 9, `items_owes_update` 11, `placement_flag_keep_row` 1, `placement_flag_owes_delete` 3, `placement_flag_owes_insert` 3, `sources_lacks_delete` 2, `sources_lacks_insert` 46, `sources_lacks_update` 6, the keyword term 13, the walk 54, the mark read as 0 56. The checks run on the tree before the change fail at the first statement (no `item_lacks`). With `recursive_triggers` on, the whole of checks/invariants.sh holds.

## Measured

io-pimdir at this change, the bench's pimbench against the same build without it, both built from io-pimdir's flake; core 12, the 517k Enron tier x1 (518,401 messages, 3,312 folders, one source `maildir`). The disk was shared with other sessions all along (/proc/pressure/io avg300 near 24%), so wall times of durable writes are read beside their CPU.

**The no-op sync**, three runs each on its own fresh store: 0.123 to 0.125 s wall, 0.103 to 0.112 s user, against 0.393 to 0.425 s wall and 0.348 to 0.363 s user without the change, the same afternoon (io-pimdir's log measured 0.49 s on an earlier day); notmuch's no-op `new` is 0.07 s. Both marks hold no row after the import (`owes` 0, `lacks` 0 over 518,401 bindings and items).

**The two statements alone**, over the 3,312 collection and source pairs, each statement prepared once and run per pair (Python's sqlite3 on SQLite 3.51.2, core 12, median of seven passes; a `SELECT 1` costs 3.2 ms): `list_owing_bindings` 432.7 ms before, 8.7 ms now; `list_lacking_items` 346.6 ms before, 12.3 ms now, 4 ms of it the guarded walk's setup (the statement without its walk: 8.3 ms). Plans on the store: `SEARCH bindings USING COVERING INDEX bindings_owing (collection=? AND source=?)`; `SEARCH i USING COVERING INDEX items_lacking (collection=?)`, the walk `SEARCH i USING INDEX sqlite_autoindex_items_1 (collection=?)` nested after `SCAN unsynced`. io-pimdir's tests/statement_scale.rs at 60,000 placements, its old texts beside: `list_owing_bindings` 28.8 ms to 0.001 ms, the same rows; `list_lacking_items` 3.8 ms both, that store holding no `sources` row, so the walk answers it.

**1,000 flag changes** (incr.sh's rename of 1,000 files of dasovich-j/all_documents to `F`, on hardlinked copies of the tree): 0.192 s wall and 0.138 s user, against 0.479 s and 0.410 s; `\Flagged` on 21,399 items in both stores after it, no mark left. The no-op after it: 0.125 to 0.138 s, against 0.391 to 0.435 s.

**The import pays for the triggers, more than 5%.** Fresh imports of the whole tier under eatmydata (fsync a no-op, so the wall time is the work), the two builds alternating, user and system seconds and wall: without the change 54.3 / 31.6 / 95.1, 54.6 / 30.5 / 95.5, 55.1 / 32.6 / 95.4, 52.5 / 30.0 / 92.0; with it 57.0 / 32.1 / 98.9, 62.7 / 37.2 / 217.7, 61.3 / 35.8 / 176.5, 57.7 / 34.0 / 99.1. The second and third runs with it began under a burst of the other sessions' I/O (pressure avg60 36 to 45%) and are left out; the two clean pairs give +4.9% user and +4.0% wall, then +10% user, +13% system and +7.7% wall, about +4% to +11% of CPU. On a 40,000-message subset, alternating, +3% user. The durable imports that built the no-op stores (1,686 s wall, 76.9 s user, 60.1 s system without the change; 1,136 s, 92.7 s, 69.3 s with its first version, before the guards below) ran at different times under that contention and compare nothing.

Where it goes, measured on a synthetic import (100,000 messages, half with a keyword row on both sides, statements prepared once, core 12): 31.8 µs a message without the triggers, 51.8 with them, 47.5 once the flag triggers skip an item no source binds and `bindings_lacks_insert` an item not marked (both kept); the owing triggers about 12 µs, the lacking ones about 7, mostly the lacking mark set at the item's insert and cleared at its binding's in the same transaction. The bytes written through `write(2)` rise 27% (70.3 to 89.5 GB) while those reaching the disk do not move (10.08 to 10.14 GB): strace on one user's folders (3,034 messages) counts the store's WAL and file writes alike with and without the change, 4,437 and 4,463, and 82,000 against 133,000 writes to SQLite's unlinked temporary files, which a statement firing a writing trigger journals into. io-pimdir runs with SQLite's default `temp_store`, so those journals are files; that is io-pimdir's tuning to weigh, not the format's.

## Implementations follow

- **io-pimdir**: spec/ re-vendored (migrations/storage/0001_init.sql and the two statements; vectors/ and payloads/ do not move), no crate code moved; it refuses a store created before this change as damaged, its schema text differing. tests/statement_scale.rs times `list_lacking_items` too.
- **The Android app** creates its store from 0001_init.sql and runs `list_owing_bindings` and `list_lacking_items` by name (PimdirStorage.java), so re-vendoring the canonical text carries both; its rust/src/offline/store.rs refuses a schema not the canonical one to the word, so a phone's store is rebuilt by a fresh sync, as for any draft. Its own statements under rust/pimdir/queries/app write items, bindings and flag rows (write_binding.sql, restate_item.sql, set_flags_known.sql, set_item_object.sql, tombstone_item.sql, mark_conflicted.sql, set_item_conflict.sql, set_base_flags_known.sql, delete_item.sql and the rest): the triggers follow them, none writes `owes` or `lacks` and none rewrites a flag row in place, which the schema now refuses. count_owing.sql spells the owing predicate again across an account's collections and could read `bindings.owes` and `items.lacks` instead. Each binding, item and flag write costs a recompute on the phone too. The triggers, views and indexes are checked on SQLite 3.18.2 by checks/floor.sh.
- **Any implementation** keeping text of its own for the schema keeps every trigger (§4.6), and a migration rebuilding `items`, `bindings`, `sources` or either flag table recreates the triggers and copies or recomputes both marks.
