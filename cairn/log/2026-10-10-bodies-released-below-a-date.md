# Bodies are released below a date, and a stated size is not the octets

The Android app's download window keeps every header and only the bodies dated inside it; moving the window later must free the bodies below it and keep the headers, so search still covers them. `collect_before` deletes whole items and `purge_item` likewise; nothing lowered an item back to `Meta`. Separately, the Graph work found that a Meta-tier size is the server's account of the message, not its octets: Graph's `PidTagMessageSize` and Exchange's `RFC822.SIZE` are Exchange's stored size, Gmail's `sizeEstimate` an estimate, while Annex A said the two tiers agree byte for byte and a listing over a held body wrote the stated size over the one the body gave.

- **`release_before(collections, until)`** (§11.4), with **`release_bases_before`** run first. Every live mail item of the set with `sort_key` below `until` (`NULL` none, the undated below any) goes back to `Meta`, its body unpinned; summary, addresses, flags, bindings and `seq` stay; each item is restamped. Guarded by invariants.sh "release: below the date, what needs no body, the undated included, two sources' bases alike", "back to Meta, flags, summary and addresses kept", "each released item moves in the feed", "a second run finds nothing" and "no ceiling reaches the newest".
- **What keeps its body.** A conflicted item or binding, a pending create, a base naming another body (a local edit), no binding, and a source of the collection not binding the item, whose propagation the body is (SYNC §9); an unpushed flag does not, needing no body, and another kind is never touched. Guarded by "release: a conflict, a diverged binding, a local edit, a pending create, no binding, an unbound source, another kind, the newer and an unnamed collection keep theirs".
- **The base goes with the body** (§11.4). For mail the base an identity keeps is the body itself, so it is released too, `base_present` set so a binding whose only witness was `base_object` does not turn into a pending create, which the second statement would then skip. Guarded by "the bases let go of the body, every binding still based, its flags kept" (it fails, and drops the witness item from the release, without `base_present`) and "a body held elsewhere stays pinned, the rest fall to the collector".
- **A released row is no claim** (SYNC §6, STORAGE §10). A `Meta` row holding its summary is listed at `Meta`, fetching, pushing and deleting nothing; the next `Full` upgrade fetches or links it. Guarded by sync vector 54.
- **A stated size may be an estimate** (Annex A.1). Without the body, `size` is what the source states; with it, the octets. The two tiers agree byte for byte save the mark and the size, and a writer holding the body writes neither read without it over it; a released row holds it no more, so the next meta restates both. Guarded by sync vectors 52 (a listing over a held body keeps 299 over a stated 312, a `Meta` row takes its stated 260), 53 (a `Full` fetch writes 241 over a stated 260) and 54 (a released row takes the stated 440 and the meta's mark); checks/vectors.py accepts `size` beside `attachment` in a member's meta and nothing else.
- **Linking** (SYNC §6) still compares the served size with the held body's octets, so a source stating an estimate links only where the two happen to agree, fetching otherwise: a missed dedup, never a wrong merge. Gmail's fetch-once across labels rests on its `sizeEstimate` equalling the raw size, unmeasured here.

A pending copy into another collection whose origin's body was released uploads its own staged body instead of copying, origins requiring the body as their base; accepted, the case being rare and lossless. Folded into draft-04, not tagged yet, so the number stays.
