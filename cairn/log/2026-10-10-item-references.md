# Items refer to each other

The ground for cross-domain links and search (the Android plan's Part C): a message to its attachment files, an invitation to its event, a mail to the contact who sent it, anything a person links by hand. A reference is a fact recorded once, so it lives in the store, where no rebuild from current content could lose one (a contact changing address keeps the links to the mail sent from the old one).

- **`item_reference`** (§4.3, §13, §14.2), folded into 0001 under §6's draft rule rather than shipped as 0002: the version stays 1 and an owner creates the table, its index and its trigger when absent on open (§6 now names tables beside indexes and triggers). Named `item_reference`, not `references`, a reserved word every statement would have to quote, and beside `item_address`. Endpoints are a collection kind and a link id, no foreign key; the key is `(from_link_id, from_kind, to_link_id, to_kind, role)`, led by the link id that the trigger seeks by, and `item_reference_to` serves the other end with the same order, so neither read sorts. Guarded by invariants.sh "reference: recorded between two held endpoints, and answered" and "read from either end".
- **Role and origin.** `attachment`, `invitation`, `sender`, `related` or `x-` and a name, compared exactly (a capital `X-` is refused, `LIKE` would fold it); `auto` or `user`. An item never refers to itself. Guarded by "an application's x- role is accepted", "a role outside the vocabulary, a bare or capital x-, and an unknown origin are refused" and "an item never refers to itself".
- **Duplicates are ignored, not refused.** Rules replay and re-match, and an idempotent `add_reference` keeps them from checking first; the plan's "duplicate refused" became "records nothing". A person's reference takes over a rule's, never the reverse, so undoing a rule's references in bulk never removes a link a person also made. `add_reference` answers the row when it recorded or took over, nothing otherwise. Guarded by "a duplicate records nothing" and "a person's takes over a rule's, never the reverse".
- **Endpoints are held** (§14.2). `add_reference` records nothing for an endpoint with no row of `items` under that kind, and the trigger `items_drop_references` deletes a reference when a delete leaves no row of either endpoint, live, tombstoned or retained. A trigger rather than the collector's sweep: the invariant "every reference's endpoints have a row" then holds after every statement and is one query to check, where a sweep leaves it false between runs and needs scheduling. Counting any row rather than the live ones keeps a reference across a tombstone and a retention (a restore finds it) and across an external move landing as a retention in one collection before the arrival in the other, which a live-row rule would lose for good. The trigger reads the reference's own kinds, since a collection deleted by cascade is gone before its items. Guarded by "an endpoint the store holds no row of, under that kind, records nothing", "a rename keeps it, and a copy deleted elsewhere too", "a tombstoned or retained last row still holds it", "the purge of the last row takes every reference of the endpoint", "the kind is in the endpoint, another kind's row under the same link id holding nothing" (it fails without the kind in the trigger), "every reference's endpoints are held, by the schema's trigger" and "a collection's delete takes the references of the endpoints it held last".
- **No feed stamp, local only.** Guarded by "stamped by SQLite, moving nothing in the feed".
- **`remove_reference`**, `references_from`, `references_to`; STORAGE §14.1 and §14.2, GUIDE §14 and OVERVIEW §2 name them. A producer's way to record one (a queue action) is left for when a frontend not owning its store needs it.

A collection whose kind is changed while it holds items leaves its references' kinds behind; kinds are declared once, and nothing guards that case. draft-04 is not tagged yet, so the number stays.
