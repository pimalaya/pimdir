# Draft 10: what an answer could not see, and the revision an index stands under

The search batch of the audit of 2026-11-12 (cairn/changes/audit-2026-11-12, decided Q12: search-v1 is tagged with S2, S4 and V7): its ledger lines S2, S4, S6 and V7, and the standard's side of S1, S3 and D5. The part stays **search-draft-10**, untagged, and requires storage-draft-10 as before; STORAGE.md and SYNC.md do not move. The index schema gains two columns of `index_meta` and one partial index, one statement is new (`set_extraction`), three change their signature (`coverage`, `init_index_meta`, `load_index_meta`), the grammar bounds two rules, and two vector formats move: vectors/search/queries.json to `v` 4 and vectors/search/store.json to `v` 2. An index a program of the earlier draft-10 text wrote differs by its schema text and is rebuilt (SEARCH §2).

## Rules and their checks

Each line names the ledger id, the rule, where it is stated and the check that fails without it. A vector value was written from the prose; none was read off an implementation, and none has been run by one yet.

- **S2, coverage counts what the index could not read** (SEARCH §8.2; GUIDE §15; OVERVIEW §10). `coverage` answers, per kind among the query's collections, four counts, each a column and a key of its own: `bodiless` (live items holding no body, from the store, as before), `missing` (placements recorded in `missing`), `unparseable` and `encrypted` (placements whose body the extraction recorded so), the last three from the index. A body filed twice counts once per placement, a kind counting nothing answers no row, a file is never counted. The partial index `object_not_ok` on `object(status, hash)` where the status is not `ok` holds the few bodies not read whole, which the statement walks to their placements. Checks, in checks/invariants.sh: "coverage: per kind, the bodiless items, the placements recorded missing ...", "coverage: a body filed twice counts once per placement ...", "coverage: a placement read at last, and one gone, are counted no more", and the plan ("... walked on object_not_ok to their placements, and missing is sought on its key ..."); the gate on indexes holds `object_not_ok` to a plan. In vectors/search/queries.json: the file's `coverage` restated in the four counts over the whole store, and eight cases stating their own.
- **S2, the query's collections** (SEARCH §8.2; GUIDE §15). Coverage was bound to "the query's collections", which no sentence defined: they are every collection of the store less those an `account:`, `collection:`, `folder:` or `kind:` term rules out, and only a term standing as a conjunct of the whole query rules any out. This states what checks/vectors.py already derived for `cut` and `horizon_left`. Check: the cases stating `coverage`, re-derived by checks/vectors.py from the store.
- **S2, a complete answer** (SEARCH §8.2; GUIDE §15; OVERVIEW §10). The standard had no definition of an answer being complete; it has one. A term reads a body when the index answers it from what the extraction recorded, and reads the bodies of some kinds alone: a text term every kind's, `date:` and `when:` a calendar item's, `has:attachment` a calendar item's, `has:phone`, `has:address`, `has:birthday`, `has:photo` and `category:` a card's, `is:encrypted` a message's, wherever the term stands, negated or not. An answer is complete when the lag is zero, the temporal axis full, no range left the horizon, no item was cut, no kind counts a bodiless item, no kind counts a `missing` or `unparseable` placement while a term reads a body of that kind, and no `encrypted` placement is counted while the query holds a text term matched in the `body` or `attachment` field (free text, `body:`, `attachment:`, `mimetype:`). A mailbox holding encrypted mail thus answers complete to a structured query and to one of header fields. Checks: sixteen cases stating `complete`, fourteen of them over two new collections of the fixture store, home/Kept (the encrypted item 12 filed a second time, and no bodiless item) and home/Lost (item 34, whose body cannot be read): a structured query, `subject:`, a role field and `is:encrypted` complete over the encrypted body, free text, `body:`, `mimetype:` and a negated `body:` not; over the missing one a structured query, a date term and `has:phone` complete, `subject:`, free text and `is:encrypted` not. checks/vectors.py re-derives each from the query's terms and the store, and requires that each of the two collections answers both ways.
- **V7, the revisions of an extraction** (SEARCH §2, §3, §6, §7; GUIDE §15; OVERVIEW §10). §6 is at revision 1, bumped by a change to what §6 extracts or to what §7 expands. `index_meta.extraction` (INTEGER) records the revision an index stands under and `index_meta.extractor` (TEXT) the indexer's own revision of what the part leaves to it, an opaque text compared byte for byte; both are written by `init_index_meta` (two new parameters), answered last by `load_index_meta`, and restated by the new `set_extraction`. An indexer finding either differing from its own extracts again every body the index holds and expands again every calendar item, leaving what a rebuild would write, and records its own; it may rebuild instead. A query client tells an indexer whose extraction is its own, for the chunk offsets, by `extractor`. Checks: the scenarios "extraction: ..." (four: recorded and answered, each restated apart, neither defaulted nor null nor of another type); queries/signatures.json; checks/names.sh for the statement. No check can hold that a change to §6 bumps the number.
- **S4, three inputs the grammar accepted and no engine answered** (SEARCH §8.1, §8.3, §8.5; GUIDE §15). The canonical compilation writes U+0000 in a text term as a space, FTS5 reading no expression past one. A span whose end lies past 9999-12-31T23:59:59Z is open on that side, and one whose start lies before 0000-01-01T00:00:00Z likewise, which the bounded relative amount still reaches. `relative` is `1*6DIGIT` and `stamp` `1*18DIGIT`, a longer one refused by the codes §8.4 had (`invalid-date`, `invalid-value`); no code is new. Checks, nine cases: `subject:weekly\u0000sync` and `stand\u0000up`; `date:today..9999` and `kind:mail date:2026-10..9999-12-31`; `kind:mail date:999999d` and `kind:contact date:999999d` accepted, `date:1000000d` refused; `changed:999999999999999999..` accepted, `changed:1000000000000000000` refused. checks/vectors.py reads the two bounds from the grammar, re-derives both refusals, compiles the first case over its FTS5 table of titles and requires that the same string with the character left in is refused or answers otherwise, and re-derives the hits of every `kind:mail` case by date, a side no four-digit year states being open.
- **S6, the zone of a query against the zone of its index** (SEARCH §8.1, §8.2; GUIDE §15; OVERVIEW §10). An answer says when the zone the query stated is another than the index's `floating_zone`, compared as names, for a query holding a date term whose collections hold a calendar one. It is reported apart and does not count against completeness; the default zone stays UTC. Checks: a case asked in Europe/Paris on the index built in UTC (`index_zone`), its two hits being the floating and the all-day event the index placed in UTC, saying `zone_differs`; a second reaching mail alone, which does not say it. checks/vectors.py re-derives the hits, events read in the index's zone and days in the query's, and `zone_differs` for every case.
- **S3, a re-expansion keeps what it cannot read** (SEARCH §4, §7; GUIDE §15; OVERVIEW §10), the standard's side. A re-expansion that cannot read an item's body keeps its occurrences and its cut and records the placement in `missing`; every pass expands again each calendar placement `load_missing_items` answers, its body indexed or not; a placement leaves `missing` only once its body was read for all the pass needed of it, the indexing and, for a calendar item, the expansion. §4's sentence "a placement whose body is indexed leaves `missing`" is restated so. Check: none here; the statements are unchanged and the rule is the indexer's order of calls. io-pimdir owes the test (a roll over an event whose blob is gone keeps its occurrences, and the next pass expands it).
- **S1, the reduction of HTML** (SEARCH §6; OVERVIEW §10), the standard's side. The reduction should separate tokens at the boundaries of block-level elements and skip head, style, script and template whatever their attributes. It stays the implementation's own and outside the hit-set guarantee (decided 2026-10-31), so no vector holds it.
- **D5, the search half** (SEARCH §9; vectors). §6 already read a field stated twice from its first occurrence; §9 now says the same of `In-Reply-To` and `References`. The gap batch 2 left, no search vector, is closed: item 33 of the fixture store is mail-repeated-fields.eml, and five cases hold that the first Subject and the first From are indexed and the second of each nowhere (`subject:second`, `from:second` and `second` answer nothing). checks/vectors.py re-derives the two `subject:` cases.

## Left

- **Whether a bodiless item counts against every query.** It does here, as io-pimdir's `is_complete` reads it today, where a `missing` placement, which the index holds as a bodiless one, counts only against a query reading a body of its kind. The same narrowing would make a headers-only mailbox answer complete to a structured query. No case states `complete` over a collection holding a bodiless item, so the owner's answer moves the sentence alone.
- **Whether a differing zone counts against completeness.** It does not: the answer saw every item, and placed some on another day. The owner may rule otherwise.
- **The open start.** The task named the end past 9999; the start before 0000 was added with it, since `1*6DIGIT` days or years before `now` lies there and no RFC 3339 instant states it.
- **`thread:` is no term that reads a body**, though a missing message's parent comes from its first `In-Reply-To` id alone, never the `References` fallback (§9), as for any bodiless one.
- **`unparseable` has no vector.** What a parser accepts is the implementation's own, so no fixture is unparseable for every implementation; the scenario holds the count.
- **A re-extraction in place has no canonical statement walking the held bodies**; an implementation writes its own or rebuilds. `set_extraction` exists so the in-place way can record what it did.
- **`coverage` walks every body not read whole**, then its placements: its cost follows the encrypted and unparseable bodies of the whole index, not of the query's collections. A person holding mostly encrypted mail pays a seek per such body per answer.
- **The year 0000.** checks/vectors.py cannot place a date in it (Python's calendar starts at 0001) and its `date_point` refuses `0000-01-01` where the grammar reads a day; it fails on a case naming that year rather than derive it wrong.
- **The consumers.** io-pimdir follows before checks/implementation.sh passes: the two columns and the index of migrations/search/0001_init.sql; `init_index_meta(version, tokenizer, extraction, extractor)`; `load_index_meta` answering `extraction` and `extractor` last; `set_extraction(extraction, extractor)`; `coverage` answering `kind, bodiless, missing, unparseable, encrypted`; the re-extraction or rebuild on either revision differing; the completeness rule and the query's collections; `zone_differs`; U+0000 as a space; the open sides; the two digit bounds; S3's order of calls and S1's reduction; and in its vector runner the `unreadable` item, `index_zone`, a case's `coverage` and `complete`. The Android app's FTS4 index owes the same but the canonical statements.
