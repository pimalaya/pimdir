# Draft 10: the payloads, identity and the derivations

The second batch of the audit of 2026-11-12 (cairn/changes/audit-2026-11-12, phase 1): the payload, identity and derivation lines of its ledger, with their vectors. The parts stay **storage-draft-10**, **sync-draft-10** and **search-draft-10**, the drafts batch 1 opened and nobody has tagged. Two vector formats move: vectors/payloads.json to `v` 2 and vectors/sync/ to `v` 5. The schema does not move, and Annex A stays at revision 3 (below).

## Rules and their checks

Each line names the ledger id, the rule, where it is stated and the check that fails without it.

- **F1, a queued flag change says what its producer read** (STORAGE §15.2, §15.3, §15.4, Annex B.1; SYNC §7; GUIDE §11, §13; OVERVIEW). `set-flags` gains an optional `base`, a flag set: with it the owner applies the current set plus what `flags` holds and `base` lacks, less what `base` holds and `flags` lacks; without it, the replace of before; a `base` met by an unknown set parks. The `SetFlags` mutation carries `base` and is refused over unknown flags. A flag change, for the capabilities, is still read against the current set. Checks: payloads/set-flags.v1.schema.json against the shape STORAGE writes; six cases of vectors/payloads.json (two valid, four malformed); sync vector 141 (a flag a sync landed after the producer read survives, and the one the producer removed goes) and 142 (refused over an unknown set, the rows unchanged); checks/vectors.py derives the set every `SetFlags` leaves from the prose.
- **F2, a queued remove on an update's rules** (STORAGE §14, §15.2, §15.3; SYNC §7, §10; GUIDE §11, §13; OVERVIEW). `remove` gains an optional `if_match` and an optional `resolves`: parked when the item holds another body than `if_match`, parked when it meets a conflict, the item's or its binding's, unless `resolves` names the `conflict_object` of each, parked when it states `resolves` and meets none. The `Remove` mutation carries both and is refused the same way, so it settles a conflict only as a person's resolution; a conflict whose diverging body nobody read is named by none, which replaces the rule that left `base_object` unknown; resolving the item's conflict it states `Settle`, which STORAGE §14 extends to a tombstone's upsert, the item keeping its body. Checks: the schema against its shape; seven payload cases; sync vector 32 restated (the conflict's body read, the remove naming it: the base adopts the revision and that body) and 143 (no resolution: refused), 144 (a resolution over an unread body: refused), 145 (an `if_match` naming a replaced body: refused), 146 (a remove naming the item's conflict settles it, body kept, bindings as they were); checks/vectors.py extends its rule on an `Edit` to a `Remove`, adds the `if_match` test for both, and checks what a settling `Remove` leaves.
- **V6, a payload is valid, malformed or unknown** (STORAGE §15.2, §15.3, §15.4, §16; GUIDE §13, §16; vectors/README.md). A payload whose action, or whose `v` for a known action, an implementation does not know is unknown to it: an owner leaves the row pending and untouched and reads none of its other members, a reader lists it and folds it into nothing. vectors/payloads.json states an `outcome` of three where a boolean stood, its `action` any non-empty string, at format `v` 2. Checks: six unknown cases (a known action at `v` 2 with `v` 1's members, with a member `v` 1 lacks, with a shape `v` 1 would refuse; an action the documents do not define; an `x-` action, twice); checks/vectors.py derives each outcome from the schemas and requires the three kinds of unknown; the scenario "skip: ..." of checks/invariants.sh (three unknown rows stay pending, payload, attempts and error untouched, while the two rows behind them apply, the drain lists them again and `load_pending_actions` still lists them), its three payloads checked to be unknown.
- **D1, the compared fields are read raw** (STORAGE §9, Annex A preamble and A.1; SYNC §4, §5, §6, §11; GUIDE §10; OVERVIEW). The contradiction is settled as decided: `subject`, `date` and `sender` are read raw, by A.1's rules, wherever the source serves them, a MUST on a connector (SYNC §4), at least for a member whose decoded summary disagrees with a row holding its hint; A.1 names each protocol's read and says plainly where a source serves nothing raw (Graph outside a message it received), a member there being named from decoded values and possibly minted apart, a missed dedup. SYNC §6 no longer says "which no server rewrites". An engine reports `Disagreed`, a new event, after the `Added` of a member minted apart from this source's own pending or delivered create, naming that create's link id. Checks: the event in vectors/schema/sync.schema.json; sync vector 111 restated with it; checks/vectors.py derives the `Disagreed` events every sync case owes and fails on one missing or spurious.
- **D3, the agreement guards a hint across collections** (STORAGE §9, §9.1, §11, §11.1, §14; SYNC §6; GUIDE §5, §11, §12; OVERVIEW). Three places, mail alone, a row of another kind agreeing with anything. Linking: `lookup_objects` answers `subject`, `date` and `sender` beside the hash and the size, one row per holder, and an upgrade adopts a body only from a row agreeing on the three and on the size where the placement states one. The shared `seq`: `seq_for_link_any(collection, link_id, subject, date, sender)` answers only a row agreeing with the summary being written, the lowest `seq` among several. The purge: `held_elsewhere`, its signature unchanged, holds a bodiless retiring message only by a row whose summary agrees with its own, which it reads itself. The last two compare the three fields alone, no body being adopted, so a `NULL` or estimated size plays no part; a row holding no summary agrees with no message. Checks: scenarios "seq: ..." (a disagreement on each field, a row with no summary, `NULL` matching `NULL`, the lowest of several, another kind untouched, the plan still a seek), "move: ..." (eleven, the plan among them) and "lookup: each holder ..."; queries/signatures.json restated; sync vectors 147 (an upgrade fetches rather than links another message of its very size, on the new fixture mail-reused-id-same-size.eml), 148 (a disagreeing member draws its own `seq`, a message filed twice shares one) and 149 (a bodiless row is retained, not purged, for another message); checks/vectors.py derives the three from the prose over every sync case.
- **D4, charset tables** (STORAGE Annex A.1; GUIDE §16; vectors/README.md). A.1 names windows-1252 behind `iso-8859-1`, says a writer carries the legacy indexes, and pins the Encoding Standard as last updated on 19 October 2026, commit snapshot be17ac5c49881336538acfc7b40dba3c8009144b. Checks: five fixtures and their cases in vectors/summaries.json (mail-charset-windows-1252.eml, which carries the decoded subject into an `alt:` key; mail-charset-iso-8859-2.eml; mail-charset-iso-2022-jp.eml; mail-charset-gbk.eml; mail-charset-replacement.eml, kept literal); checks/vectors.py decodes the three new encodings by Python's codecs only over octets where they provably agree with the standard's decoders and fails on any other. Avoided: for ISO-2022-JP everything but `ESC $ B` and `ESC ( B` over rows 4, 5 and 16 to 84 (so JIS X 0201 Roman and katakana, the 1978 set, rows 1 and 2 where five symbols map to fullwidth forms, row 13 and the extension rows); for GBK the octet `80`, four-octet sequences and every pair outside GB 2312's character rows; ISO-8859-2 agrees whole.
- **D5, a field stated twice** (STORAGE Annex A.1; SEARCH §6; GUIDE §10). The first occurrence in document order, whichever column reads it; JMAP's raw read is `header:X:asRaw:all`, first element, never the plain form, which answers the last. SEARCH §6 binds the extraction to A.1's reading of a field. Check: mail-repeated-fields.eml and its case (key, subject, sender, its one address row and date are the first's).
- **D6, a usable hint** (STORAGE §9; GUIDE §11, §16). Usable means not empty once trimmed of the space and the tab, no other character; a usable hint is the key verbatim. Checks: card-empty-uid.vcf and event-blank-uid.ics, keyed by the `hash:` fallback, their `uid` column verbatim; checks/vectors.py reads usability so.
- **D7, a resource of overrides alone** (STORAGE Annex A.3, A.4 and A.5 inheriting). With no master, the first component carrying a `RECURRENCE-ID` in document order gives the `uid`, the summary, the times, the addresses and the sort key; `recurring` and `until` read it too, so `0` and `NULL` for an override stating no rule. Check: a case on event-override-only.ics; checks/vectors.py reads the master, else the first override, where it raised.
- **D8, the fold and the trim of a sort key** (STORAGE Annex A.2; vectors/README.md). Final_Sigma applies and no other conditional mapping; the trim is the White_Space property of Unicode 15.0.0, listed. Checks: card-final-sigma.vcf (a sigma before a space, at the end of the name and before the `@` of an address) and card-nbsp-name.vcf; checks/vectors.py trims by that set, no longer by `str.strip()`.
- **D9, a field's name** (STORAGE Annex A.1, A.7). An unfolded line splits at its first colon, the name trimmed of trailing whitespace, a line with no colon no field. Check: mail-spaced-field-name.eml (`Message-ID :` is found and keys the message). Three sentences besides, on the reference's reading: an RFC 2231 value in a charset no label names is read as UTF-8 (A.7); a `UID` line longer than 65,536 octets once unfolded is read by no writer, the invitation `NULL` (A.1); a raw field carried in JSON owes the byte-for-byte agreement only where its bytes are UTF-8 (A.1).

## Left

- **F2, a move over a conflict.** SYNC §7 says a `Move` "also tombstones the source" and nothing more. By §3 and STORAGE §14 its tombstone leaves both conflicts standing; io-pimdir's write clears an item's conflict on any tombstone upsert (src/hub.rs:455), so a queued `move` still discards a diverging body unseen there. Nothing was written for it: whether a `move` meeting a conflict parks, as a `remove` now does, or stages its tombstone under the conflict, is the owner's to decide, with a vector.
- **F2, `if_match` over an item holding no body.** The rule is an `update`'s, "another body". io-pimdir refuses when the item holds none too; the text does not say, for either action.
- **V6, a payload stating no `v`**, or one that is no integer, for a known action: by the letter it names no schema and is unknown, never parked. No case states it.
- **D1, how a connector learns of the row its member disagrees with.** The seam hands a connector no store; SYNC §4 leaves the means to it and notes that reading every member raw always meets the rule. An engine-side fetch on a disagreement would need a member saying its summary is decoded, a seam change nobody decided.
- **D3, files of parts.** Two messages under one `Message-ID` in two collections each stand for their attachments under `part:`, the link id, `#` and the section, in one file collection: the second's stand-ins meet the first's keys. Outside the three places decided, so left.
- **D3, kinds in `held_elsewhere`.** A bodied retiring row is held by a row of any kind holding its body, as before.
- **D5, the search half.** SEARCH §6's sentence has no case under vectors/search/: adding a message to the search store moves most of its 179 cases, which only an implementation re-derives.
- **D9's three sentences** carry no fixture: the standard library's reading of an RFC 2231 name and of a part past 64 KiB is the second path of checks/vectors.py, and would not be an independent one there.
- **Annex A stays at revision 3.** What moved is read so by io-pimdir already (the field name, the first occurrence, the first override, the fold and the trim), but for a blank `UID`, which moves a key, and a key is never re-derived; a draft's store is read under its draft alone. - **D2 and D10** are the consumers' and io-pimdir's.
