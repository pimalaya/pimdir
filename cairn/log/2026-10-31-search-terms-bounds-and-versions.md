# Search: text terms, bounds, versions and what leaves the index

Batch 6 of the release audit (cairn/changes/audit-2026-10-31, lines Q1 to Q7, and G7's HTML part), with the three notes earlier batches left for the search part: the index's own `object.hash`, a body refetched under its name after a quarantine, and `store_reconciled` beside the purge count a rename now moves. storage-draft-08, sync-draft-07 and search-draft-08 stay as batch 1 set them; Annex A stays at revision 3, which this draft has not released, its walk bound folded into it.

Rules, and the check guarding each. Every check below was run against the tree before this batch (the working tree after batch 5, copied with `cp -a`): checks/invariants.sh with errexit off, so each scenario reports, checks/names.sh as it stands now, and the earlier checks/vectors.py over the new vectors; each failed there unless the line calls it a guard. Where the check is a re-derivation in checks/vectors.py, the rule was also removed from a copy of the finished tree, one at a time, and vectors.py refused the copy; each seed is named on its line.

## Measurements

FTS5, 300,000 bodies of 60 random words, 97 % holding a common word and 0.1 % a rare one, SQLite 3.50.4, warm, tmpfs: every match of the common word (291,120 rows, no join) 0.214 s; `ORDER BY rank LIMIT 50` 0.180 s, bm25 being computed for every match before the page is kept; 1,000 probes of one rowid each (`MATCH … AND rowid = ?`) 0.022 s for the common word and 0.016 s for the rare one. A page of 50 hits walked in the store's order thus costs about 51 probes for a common term, 1 ms, and about 50,000 for the rare one, 0.8 s, where reading the rare term's 311 matches first costs nothing: the two forms of §8.2 suit opposite terms, and the ranked form bounds what reaches the client rather than what FTS5 computes.

`coverage`, on batch 3's synthetic store rebuilt under this schema (1,270,041 items, 811,342 of them live and bodiless: 611,301 messages at `Meta` and 200,041 stand-ins), SQLite 3.50.4, warm: the earlier statement, store-wide on the whole `items_by_object`, 16,522,565 VM steps, 0.19 s; per collection on `items_bodiless`, a1/INBOX alone 2,381,276 VM, 0.022 s, and all thirteen collections 7,947,191 VM, 0.071 s, the stand-ins' collections left out by kind before any seek. A count still reads each bodiless row in scope. Sizes: `items_by_object` whole 34.7 MB; partial 30.5 MB beside `items_bodiless` 19.5 MB, +15.2 MB, 0.7 % of the 2,111 MB store.
## Rules

- **A text term is matched on its tokens, never spliced** (SEARCH §8.5, §8, §8.3, queries/search/match_objects.sql and match_summaries.sql; Q1). A text, a phrase and a text field's value are text terms: folded (§5), split by the index's tokenizer, matched as consecutive tokens in order in the fields §8's table maps the term to and no other, a trailing wildcard making the last token a prefix; no character of the term is engine syntax and a term yielding no token matches nothing. The canonical shape compiles each to one FTS5 string, `"` doubled, ` *` for the wildcard alone, behind `{column} : ` for a field term, a field `summary_text` lacks not run against summaries. Before, the compilation was unstated: `{body} : ^first` and `{body} : "^first"` answer differently on FTS5, and a raw splice errs (`{title} : invitation: weekly`, `+weekly`, `{weekly}`, `weekly-sync`) or matches what the phrase excludes (`weekly AND sync`, `NEAR(weekly sync)`, `week*`). Guarded by ten new query cases (`subject:^sync`, `title:^sy*`, `title:+weekly`, `subject:{weekly}`, `subject:weekly-sync`, `subject:"invitation: weekly"`, `subject:"weekly AND sync"`, `subject:"NEAR(weekly sync)"`, `subject:"week*"`, `body:^report`) and by checks/vectors.py, which now builds an FTS5 table of the fixture store's titles (a message's decoded Subject, a card's FN, N and NICKNAME, a component's SUMMARY, a summary row), folded, compiles every case whose query is one term on the title field as §8.5 says and compares its hits (twelve cases), and refuses a case holding FTS5 syntax whose text spliced raw answers alike. The earlier vectors.py checked the ten for grammar alone and passed them. Seeds: the compiler replaced by a raw splice (refused on the nine title cases: six answer other hits, four FTS5 refuses), `subject:^sync` expecting nothing (refused), `subject:"week*"` rewritten as `subject:"weekly + sync"`, which a splice answers alike (refused). io-pimdir's `fts` (src/client/search/compile.rs) already quotes the whole folded term, doubles `"` and stars a prefix after the string, so it answers the new cases.
- **The walk of a message's parts is bounded** (STORAGE Annex A.1, its revision 3 note, SEARCH §6; Q2). The walk reads entities in document order, a multipart before its children, the message at depth 0, a `message/rfc822` part one entity never opened, none deeper than 64 and none after its 10,000th, the message's own counted; an entity left unread with its parts makes no mark, invitation, stand-in nor extracted text. Depth 64 is io-pimdir's `MIME_DEPTH_MAX`, with the same reading; 10,000 entities is Dovecot's default `max_total_mime_parts`. The `message/rfc822` reading is what io-pimdir and vectors.py already did, now written. Guarded by vectors/summaries.json, two new cases: mail-deep-parts.eml (an attachment at depth 64, stood for at section `1.1…1`, beside one at depth 65, not) and mail-many-parts.eml (9,998 bare parts, then an attachment as the 10,000th entity, stood for at section `9999`, and one as the 10,001st, not), and checks/vectors.py, whose walk now holds both bounds and which re-derives the mark with the body (`attachment`) from it for every mail case, where before it re-derived only the mark without the body. The earlier vectors.py, walking unbounded, failed both cases. Seeds: the depth bound lifted, the count bound lifted (each refused).
- **At most 1,000 stand-ins per message** (STORAGE §14.3; Q2). The first 1,000 parts making the mark, in walk order, are stood for and no later one; a reader finding 1,000 reads the rest from the body. Guarded by summaries.json's mail-many-attachments.eml (1,001 attachments, 1,000 stood for, the mark 1) and vectors.py's walk. The earlier vectors.py failed it; seed: the bound lifted (refused).
- **Occurrences and recurrence rules are bounded, and a cut is reported** (SEARCH §7, §8.2, §11; `placement.cut`, `placement_cut`, `set_cut`, `cut_before`; Q2). An item writes at most 10,000 occurrences, the first by start then end of those overlapping the horizon; an expander examines at most 1,000,000 periods of a rule, a period being `INTERVAL` times `FREQ` from the one holding `DTSTART`, before the horizon included, whether or not it yields; a `VTIMEZONE` rule on the same bound keeps its last onset's offset and cuts nothing. An item a bound stopped records its cut, the earlier of its 10,001st occurrence's start and its first unexamined period's; `cut_before` counts, among the query's collections, the items cut before a range's end, which a dated answer's coverage reports. A daily series over the default three-year horizon writes 1,096 occurrences and reaches its horizon from 2,739 years back; io-pimdir's `OCCURRENCES_MAX` of 1,000 cut it. Guarded by two new store items, event-hourly.ics (`FREQ=HOURLY` from 1 February 2027: 10,000 occurrences, cut at 2028-03-23T16:00:00Z) and event-sparse.ics (`FREQ=SECONDLY` limited to 1 August at midnight: its `DTSTART`, cut at its millionth second, 2026-08-12T13:46:40Z), two occurrence windows, four cases stating `cut`, and checks/vectors.py, which reads both bounds from §7's prose and expands a simple rule (one `VEVENT`, a UTC start, a `DURATION`, a fixed step, limiting `BYxxx` parts) for the windows and the `cut` counts, and requires every other calendar item of the store to state `UNTIL` or `COUNT`. The earlier vectors.py refused the `cut` key. Seeds: the occurrence bound raised to 100,000 (refused: the window holds no row past the cut, two counts move), the period bound raised to 100,000,000 (refused: the sparse series is no longer cut, three counts move), a `cut` of 1 where 2 is due (refused). By invariants.sh, "cut: an item cut before a range's end counts in its collections…" and "cut: an item expanded again within the bounds is no longer counted, the count seeking placement_cut" (no statement before).
- **A query is bounded** (SEARCH §8.3's limits table, §8.4's `limit-exceeded`; Q2). 1,024 Unicode scalar values, 32 groups open at once, 128 terms; refused as `limit-exceeded`, the length in a first pass whatever else the query holds, the nesting in the parentheses pass beside an unbalanced parenthesis (the leftmost answering), the count when the reader reaches the 129th term, before its own checks. The reference parser recursed per group and overflowed at about 300. Guarded by eight new cases, at and one past each bound, the length refused over an unterminated phrase, an unbalanced parenthesis left of the 33rd group answering first, and checks/vectors.py, whose parser reads the three numbers from §8.3's table and refuses the nesting before it recurses. The earlier vectors.py accepted every case past a bound and knew no `limit-exceeded`. Seeds: each bound doubled in the table (refused: the case one past it accepted).
- **An indexer rebuilds only an older index of its own shape** (SEARCH §2, §3; `index_meta.format_version`, `shape`; `init_index_meta`, `load_index_meta`; Q3). Every index, whatever its shape, holds `index_meta` with `format`, `format_version` (`search-draft-08`, then `search-vN`) and `shape` (`canonical`, or the implementation's own name). Versions are ordered, drafts by number below every release. An indexer refuses an index.db with no `index_meta`, of another shape or a later version, never rebuilding, writing nor deleting it; rebuilds one of its own shape at an earlier version or lacking `format_version`; and, at its own, rebuilds on the tokenizer, the schema text, the store or the cursor as before. A query client refuses an index whose shape or version it does not implement, naming both, a draft implementing its own draft alone as STORAGE §4.2 has it. The schema text no longer tells drafts apart. Guarded by invariants.sh, "index: init_index_meta stamps this draft and the canonical shape, which load_index_meta answers", and by checks/names.sh, which fails when the version `init_index_meta` writes is not SEARCH.md's Status version (before: none written). The ordering, the refusals and the rebuild are a program's behaviour no SQL observes.
- **Floating times are read in a recorded zone** (SEARCH §7; `index_meta.floating_zone`, `set_floating_zone`, `items_to_reexpand`'s `:floating_zone`; Q4). The indexer states an IANA zone, `UTC` when none; `floating_zone` records it, `UTC` at creation; an indexer stating another re-expands every live calendar item and records its own in that pass, as a new time zone database does. Guarded by invariants.sh, "zone: an index reads floating times in UTC until an indexer states another", "zone: an indexer stating another zone re-expands every live calendar item…" and "zone: once recorded, the zone re-expands nothing more, and UTC is another zone again". The vectors state no zone, so an indexer runs them in `UTC` (§11, GUIDE §16).
- **A message that leaves prunes its thread** (SEARCH §9, §4; `unparent_messages`, `prune_messages`, `thread_rows`, `delete_thread`, `placements_gone` answering the link id; Q5). A member is a message a live mail placement holds; a `message` row exists for every id a member bears or names, and no other. When a mail placement that held its link id last goes, by a deleted row or the key reconciliation, its thread's rows no member holds name no parent, those no row names go, and each connected set left is re-keyed to its lowest present member, so the rows are those a rebuild writes. Guarded by invariants.sh, "thread: a message no placement holds names no parent, and goes once no row names it, its phantom parent with it", "thread: thread_rows reads each row's parent and a held one's sort key…", "thread: what is left is what a rebuild from the store as it stands writes…" and "thread: the prune seeks the thread's rows, never every message"; "thread: a thread row a member still names is kept" is a guard.
- **SEARCH names every statement** (SEARCH §3's table by profile: indexer, query client, both; Q6). 54 statements, each with its section, binding on STORAGE §4.4's terms. Guarded by checks/names.sh, which fails unless the table names exactly the files under queries/search/ with a profile of the three (before: no table, 41 statements named only where a document mentioned them).
- **Answers can be bounded** (SEARCH §8.2 "A page", §8.5; `coverage` with the `collections` slot, `match_objects_ranked`, `match_object`, `match_summary`; STORAGE §4.3 and 0001_init.sql: `items_by_object` partial, `items_bodiless` new; Q7). `coverage` counts the query's collections by a covering seek of `items_bodiless(collection, deleted, object_hash) WHERE object_hash IS NULL AND deleted = 0`, so `items_by_object` joins the other pointer indexes, partial on the pointer set. A client MAY answer a page: walking the store's pages and probing each row's text terms (`match_object`, `match_summary`), or reading a rare term first; by relevance, `match_objects_ranked` reads a page of best bodies, FTS5 ordering by rank. Guarded by invariants.sh, "coverage: a bodiless file hides nothing…", "coverage: only the query's collections are counted", "coverage: each collection seeks items_bodiless, never the store's other rows", "ranked: a page of the bodies a term matches best…", "ranked: FTS5 orders by rank and keeps the page, sorting nothing itself", "probe: one body, or one summary, tested against a term", "probe: each seeks the term's matches by rowid, never every match", and "schema: a pointer index holds the set pointers alone, items_by_object too, and items_bodiless the rest…"; by checks/floor.sh, whose object delete still opens the six pointer indexes alone on SQLite 3.18.2 with `items_by_object` partial (a guard). The plans of the 199 store statements are unchanged by the two indexes (diffed before and after); `item_reference_collects_files` keeps its `+object_hash IS NULL`, its comment updated.
- **The index names a body as the store does** (migrations/search/0001_init.sql; batch 2's note). `object.hash` and `placement.hash` carry the store's `CHECK (length(hash) = 52 AND hash NOT GLOB '*[^a-z2-7]*')`. Guarded by invariants.sh, "index: an object and a placement name a body as the store does…" (before: `../x` stored). The harness names index bodies through `name` like the store's.
- **A body that cannot be read is tried again** (SEARCH §4; the `missing` table, `insert_missing`, `load_missing_items`, `delete_missing`; batch 2's note). A body missing or failing its name is indexed bodiless and recorded; every pass first folds the recorded placements again, the store stamping nothing when a quarantined body is fetched again under its name; one indexed or bodiless leaves, one deleted goes by cascade. Guarded by invariants.sh, "missing: a placement whose body could not be read is folded again by the next pass, though the store stamped nothing", "missing: a placement whose body is indexed leaves it" and "missing: a placement deleted takes its row".
- **The purge count alone reconciles keys** (SEARCH §4; `index_meta.store_reconciled` removed, `set_index_cursor` without `:store_reconciled`, `load_index_meta`; batch 1's note). A rename counts in `store_meta.purges` (STORAGE §4.5), and a batch never moves `store_purges`, so a rename a cut pass folded past still reconciles at the next pass; the separate high-water mark is gone. Guarded by invariants.sh, "reconciled: past a batch, the store's purge count still stands above the one the index recorded, so the next pass reconciles the old id" (before: `set_index_cursor` refused a row without `store_reconciled`, so nothing moved).
- **HTML is outside the hit-set guarantee** (SEARCH §2, §6; G7's HTML part). The reduction of `text/html` is an implementation's own, unpinned, and a word only that text holds MAY be a hit in one conforming index and not another; the vectors hold no such word. Of the two options the ledger offered, exclusion: pinning a reduction an HTML parser makes byte-identical across implementations is a specification of its own. A scoping statement no check can observe; no vector moved.
